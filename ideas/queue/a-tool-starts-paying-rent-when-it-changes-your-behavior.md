## Hook

A side project can have elegant code, complete docs, and a release artifact without earning a place in anyone's work. The stronger signal is behavioral: what decision, habit, or failure became different after the tool existed?

## Thesis

The value of an internal tool is best measured by the repeated behavior it changes, not the feature list it accumulates.

## Reader Value

### Engineers

A practical filter for deciding whether to deepen, simplify, or stop building a tool.

### Decision-makers

A way to evaluate internal tooling through adoption and changed outcomes instead of development effort.

## Evidence

- Godmode changing how work persists across sessions.
- Obfsck changing what can safely leave a machine.
- Devloop changing how recent engineering work is reconstructed.
- Counterexamples from prototypes that never altered a recurring workflow.

## Mini Outline

1. Contrast a polished unused prototype with a rough tool used every day.
2. Define behavioral evidence: frequency, changed decisions, prevented failures, and replaced rituals.
3. Compare several tools against that standard.
4. Turn the observations into stop, maintain, and invest criteria.
